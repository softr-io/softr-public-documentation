Google Sign-in puts a **Continue with Google** button on your app's sign-in and sign-up forms. People who already have a Google account get in with one click, and you never hold a password for them.

Setting it up happens in two places: you create an OAuth client in the Google Cloud console, then paste its client ID and secret into Softr. Keep both tabs open — you will be moving between them.

<Note>
  Google Sign-in is one of the methods on the [sign-in form](/add-and-manage-users/signin-form), alongside email and password. It is not the same as [SSO](/add-and-manage-users/single-sign-on-overview), which hands authentication to your own identity provider over SAML or OpenID. You can switch both on at once.
</Note>

## Before you start

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="A Google account that can create Cloud projects">
    Creating a project needs the `resourcemanager.projects.create` permission. Personal Google accounts have it; a work account in a Google Cloud organization often does not, and the **No organization** row in the project picker will be marked with a warning triangle. If that happens, ask whoever administers your Google Cloud organization to create the project — or to grant you the permission — and carry on from step 2.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Your app's URL, exactly as visitors see it">
    Open your published app and copy the address out of the browser bar. On a Softr subdomain that is `https://yourapp.softr.app`, with no `www.`; on a [custom domain](/publishing/add-a-custom-domain-to-your-app) it is whatever host you connected. You need the scheme and host only — no trailing slash, no path.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Create your Google OAuth client

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create a Google Cloud project">
    In the [Google Cloud console](https://console.cloud.google.com/projectcreate), give the project a name. Google derives a project ID from it, which cannot be changed later. If your account belongs to an organization you also have to pick a **Parent resource**. Click **Create**.

    <Frame caption="Naming a new project in the Google Cloud console.">
      ![The New Project form with a project name filled in and the Create button below it](/images/google-sign-in/create-google-cloud-project.png)
    </Frame>

    <Tip>
      Already have a project for this app? Skip this step and pick it from the project picker in the console's top bar instead.
    </Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name the app people will consent to">
    Go to **Google Auth Platform → Branding**. **App name** and **User support email** are what people see on Google's consent screen the first time they sign in, so use the name of your portal rather than the project ID. Click **Save**.

    <Frame caption="App name and support email on the Branding page.">
      ![The App information section of the Branding page, with the app name and user support email fields outlined](/images/google-sign-in/branding-app-information.png)
    </Frame>

    <Note>
      On a brand-new project, opening Google Auth Platform starts a short **Get started** wizard that asks for the app name, support email, audience and a contact address in one pass. Complete it and you arrive at these pages with the work already done. Google documents the rest of the branding options in [Manage OAuth App Branding](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/15549049).
    </Note>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose who can sign in — and publish the app">
    Open **Audience**. Two settings on this page decide who gets in.

    <Frame caption="Publishing status and user type on the Audience page.">
      ![The Audience page showing publishing status Testing and user type External](/images/google-sign-in/audience-publishing-status.png)
    </Frame>

    1. **Publishing status.** A new project starts in **Testing**, and while it is in Testing only the Google accounts listed under **Test users** can sign in — up to 100 of them. Click **Publish app** to move it into production.
    2. **User type.** **External** lets anyone with a Google account sign in. **Internal** appears only if your Google account belongs to a Google Workspace organization, and limits sign-in to that organization.

    <Warning>
      Leaving the project in **Testing** is the most common reason a correctly configured Google Sign-in still fails: everyone who is not on the test-user list is refused. Publish the app before you point real users at it. Google's [Manage App Audience](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/15549945) covers the limits in full.
    </Warning>

    Note which **User type** you picked. You set the same value on the Softr side.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create the OAuth client">
    Go to **Clients → Create client**. Set **Application type** to **Web application** and give the client a name. The name is only used to identify the client in the console; nobody signing in ever sees it.

    <Frame caption="Choosing the Web application client type.">
      ![The Create OAuth client ID form with application type set to Web application and a client name entered](/images/google-sign-in/create-oauth-client-type.png)
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add your app's URL to both lists">
    Under **Authorized JavaScript origins**, click **Add URI** and paste your app's URL. Then do the same under **Authorized redirect URIs**. Click **Create**.

    <Frame caption="The same app URL added as a JavaScript origin and a redirect URI.">
      ![The two URI lists on the OAuth client form, each holding the app's URL](/images/google-sign-in/oauth-client-uris.png)
    </Frame>

    <Note>
      Match the origin your visitors actually load, character for character. A Softr subdomain has no `www.` — `https://acme-portal.softr.app`, not `https://www.acme-portal.softr.app`, which does not resolve. On a custom domain, include `www.` only if that is the host people land on.
    </Note>

    <Tip>
      Google registers the domains of these URIs as **Authorized domains** on your consent screen automatically, so there is no separate step for that. Changes to a client can take anywhere from five minutes to a few hours to take effect.
    </Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Copy the client ID and secret">
    Google shows the new client's ID and secret as soon as it is created. Copy both before you close the panel.

    <Warning>
      Google no longer lets you view a client secret after creation — the client's page shows only its last four characters. If you lose it, open the client and use **Add secret** to issue a new one, then update Softr with the new value.
    </Warning>
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Add the credentials to Softr

<Steps>
  <Step title="Switch Google Sign-in on">
    In Softr Studio, open your app and go to **Users → Authentication**. Turn on the **Google Sign-in** toggle (1), then click the gear (2) to open its settings.

    <Frame caption="The Google Sign-in row in the Authentication method list.">
      ![The Authentication method list with the Google Sign-in toggle switched on and its gear icon beside it](/images/google-sign-in/enable-google-sign-in.png)
    </Frame>

    <Note>
      An amber dot next to a method means **Setup is incomplete** — it is switched on but has no working configuration yet. It clears once you save valid credentials.
    </Note>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Paste the credentials">
    Paste the **Google client ID** and **Google client secret** you copied from the console (1). Then set **User type** (2) to the same value you chose on the Audience page.

    <Frame caption="The Google Sign-in settings dialog with Internal selected.">
      ![The Google Sign-in settings dialog showing the client ID and secret fields, the user type control, and the email domains field](/images/google-sign-in/google-sign-in-settings.png)
    </Frame>

    With **Internal**, a **Google Sign-in email domains** field appears. Anyone whose email address is on one of these domains is always sent to Google to log in, even when your app also offers email or SSO. Choosing **External** hides the field, because an external client accepts any Google account.

    <Frame caption="With External selected, the email domains field is not shown.">
      ![The same dialog with user type set to External and no email domains field beneath it](/images/google-sign-in/google-sign-in-settings-external.png)
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save and publish">
    Click **Save**, then publish your app so the change reaches the live site. The amber dot beside **Google Sign-in** should disappear.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What people see

**Continue with Google** appears above the email field on both the sign-in and the [sign-up form](/add-and-manage-users/signup-form). The first time someone uses it, Google shows a consent screen carrying the app name and support email you set under **Branding**.

<Frame caption="The Continue with Google button on a published app's login page.">
  ![An app login page with Continue with Google above the SSO button and the email field](/images/google-sign-in/continue-with-google.png)
</Frame>

Signing in this way creates or matches a record in your app's [users list](/add-and-manage-users/users-menu) on the email address the Google account carries.

## Troubleshooting

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Google refuses the request before showing the account chooser">
    The URL registered on the OAuth client does not match the address the browser is on. Compare them character for character: `http` against `https`, `www.` present or absent, and no trailing slash. If you have just changed the client, give it a few minutes — Google warns that client changes can take up to a few hours to propagate.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Some people can sign in and others cannot">
    Either the Google Cloud project is still in **Testing**, in which case only the accounts on the test-user list are allowed, or the client's **User type** is **Internal** and the people being refused are outside your Google Workspace organization. Both live on the **Audience** page.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The amber dot beside Google Sign-in will not clear">
    Softr marks a method **Setup is incomplete** until it has a client ID and secret saved. Reopen the gear and check both fields are filled and saved.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Only people on certain domains are sent to Google">
    That is the **Google Sign-in email domains** field doing its job. Clear it if you want those people to be able to use email or SSO as well.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
